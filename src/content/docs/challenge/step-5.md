Clients do not buy "repositories." They buy "case studies." A case study shows a prospective client that you understand problems and have a track record of delivering business results. In this step, you write a structured case study for a past project.

---

## 🎯 Step Objectives
*   Understand the Challenge-Solution-Outcome case study framework.
*   Draft a case study summarizing a technical project or issue you have resolved.
*   Prepare the case study to be shared with prospects.

---

## 🏃‍♂️ Action Guide

### 1. Case Study Framework (CSO)
Do not describe your codebase line-by-line. Instead, divide your case study into three clear sections:

1.  **The Challenge (The Context & Friction):**
    What was the operational bottleneck?
    *Example: A local restaurant was losing reservations because customers had to call during busy lunch hours. If the phone was busy, clients left.*
2.  **The Solution (The Technical Execution):**
    How did you solve the problem using software? Avoid overly dense technical jargon. Focus on structure.
    *Example: Built a responsive React booking landing page. Integrated Twilio Webhooks to automatically send confirmation SMS messages to customers' phones.*
3.  **The Outcome (The Business Results):**
    Highlight positive results or speed metrics. If you do not have live client data, use performance speedups (e.g., LCP load times, API response reductions, developer productivity).
    *Example: Deployed the site using Vite & Vercel, achieving a 100% lighthouse performance score and reducing average booking registration times to under 30 seconds.*

### 2. Draft the Case Study
Create a document (Markdown file, Google Doc, or Notion page) containing your drafted case study. Keep it clear, concise, and focused on business value. You will link this document in your personal launcher profile card.
